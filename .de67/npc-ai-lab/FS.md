## The developer's route

Choose or author a named encounter, fix a seed and the starting conditions, then run it headless. See what the actors achieved and which ordinary turns cost time. Click a hitch or a suspected wriggle, watch the approach and marked turn in Tiles, and inspect the actor, goal, decision, attempted action and result. Change one obstacle, loadout or code revision and compare from the first unequal turn. A timing comparison remains bound to its headless runs. A playable intervention gets its own history.

### Encounter and turn ownership

- [ ] 🔴 R-LAB-ENCOUNTER — Author, vary and repeat an encounter through one lab entry point.
  - The existing harness registry, scenario JSON and fixture bindings provide named cases and run provenance. The selected production launcher in `src/main.cpp` already supports a world name, scenario and raw seed. `src/main_menu.cpp` prepares the overmap seed before world start. `game::prepare_harness_overmap_seed` and `rng_set_engine_seed` cover particular RNG boundaries. In particular, a zero argument to the latter leaves the current stream in place.
  - A versioned encounter declares seed, world or fixture identity, terrain and obstacle layout, actor placements, group/faction, actual equipment and ammunition, creature species/count, initial goals, player starting position and ordinary player input schedule, and run horizon. Use native game IDs and coordinates with their units. Resolve randomized setup and expose the effective start, including data/mods, options and build. Give single runs and seed/parameter batches the same entry point and recipe. Bind each output to a distinct run before starting. A rerun begins with its own disposable game state.
  - Validate unknown IDs, impossible placements and incompatible starts before scoring. A seed that cannot be applied reproducibly must be rejected or given an explicitly defined reproducible mapping. Check the effective start after native loadout and map creation. After setup, only normal simulation owners can move actors, choose tactics or pay travel time.
  - **Proof.** From the public lab entry, save and reopen a case, vary each requested dimension, and inspect the resulting actors and map. Repeating the same seed and effective recipe recreates the start and subsequent normalized turn sequence. Alter one input and identify the changed starting fact. Reject an invalid roster or blocked spawn with a useful field error. Mass execution returns individually reproducible results, including abnormal termination.

- [ ] 🔴 R-LAB-TURN — Headless and Tiles run the same ordinary native encounter.
  - In `src/main.cpp` the game loop calls `game::do_turn()`. In `src/do_turn.cpp` an advancing turn increments the clock, refreshes activation, runs world services, processes avatar input/activity and world work, calls `monmove()`, and runs `overmap_npc_move()` on its cadence. `process_monsters_and_npcs_turn_for_test()` only refreshes activation and calls `monmove()`. Directly calling either inner motor omits clock and world ownership.
  - Deliver the encounter's waiting or movement inputs through normal avatar actions. Drive the ordinary advancing-turn boundary in both modes. Input cadence, avatar position, starting time, supported systems/options and world activity must agree. Headless mass execution requires no manual input or displayed Tiles window. Pausing after a turn preserves the next input; stepping completes exactly one advancing turn even if one input spans multiple turns or one turn takes several actions. A new-game initialization pass, quit, crash or partial turn has its own status.
  - **Proof.** Run both anchors headless and in Tiles. Compare per-turn actors, player, activation footprint, positions, goals, decisions, paid travel and outcomes through a due overmap update. Record initial and terminal turns and any incomplete interval. Run several seeds in a batch and rerun one alone to catch leaking global RNG, actor counters or cached state.

### Actor behavior and reality-bubble continuity

- [ ] 🔴 R-LAB-STALKER — Explain progress and wriggling in a local writhing stalker encounter.
  - Use the native `mon_writhing_stalker` with real targets, cover or obstacles and adjustable surrounding creatures. `monmove()` drives the monster loop, with `monster::plan()`, `monster::move()` in `src/monmove.cpp` and typed state in `src/writhing_stalker_ai.{h,cpp}`. The existing `writhing_stalker.live_hit_fade_retreat_mcw.json` gives a strike/disengage starting point. Record the actual tactic, chosen target and position, attempted step/action, actual before/after position, outcome and blocking reason where the owner knows it.
  - Inspect successive opportunities to act. Detect a repeating position cycle or attempts without goal progress in a scenario-defined window. Show target/goal changes, travel credit, damage or other effective actions and the reason for a hold or withdrawal. Track progress appropriate to the intent, including separation after a strike. Allow uncertainty when an owner supplied insufficient evidence. A marker identifies a candidate for inspection.
  - **Proof.** Exercise a real obstructed/repeated-attempt case and a purposeful withdrawal or legitimate wait. Click the suspect turn and distinguish their histories, including equal positions with different tactical results. Show which actors actually acted as creature count and obstacles change. Never award a scripted strike, retreat, stall classification or route outcome.

- [ ] 🔴 R-LAB-CARAVAN — Carry a well-armed faction caravan through city travel, local AI and repeated handoffs.
  - The Free Merchants caravan definitions in `data/json/npcs/random_encounters/free_merchant_caravans.json` give relevant loadout/classes and stationary jobs. `talk_function::caravan_depart` in `src/mission_companion.cpp` sets mission timing. Neither supplies evidence of this moving party. Bind the recipe to real, persistently identifiable faction NPCs, inspect their actual gear, and give them a city route and goal that the normal travel owner can service. The specific faction and route are encounter inputs.
  - `overmap_npc_move()` considers the avatar's surrounding OMTs and specialized party reservations. `overmap_party_travel::prepare_transit` prepares route/paid work. Its callers authenticate and commit bodies and shifts. `game::update_map`, `load_npcs`, `unload_npcs`, and the local creature tracker control the active map and bodies. For structural outings, `materialize_live_bandit_structural_handoffs`, `dematerialize_live_bandit_structural_handoffs` and `bandit_live_world::commit_local_pair_handoff` are existing specialized owners; their `local_handoff_snapshot` records member and route facts. Use the actual chosen faction's owner, and explicitly establish its support for the selected roster. A generic caravan cannot inherit a structural scout's proof.
  - First exercise an entirely offscreen leg with the player’s reality bubble away from the party. Watch coarse position, travel credit and route advance on native time without opening the remote map to force progress. In another matched case, normal prescribed avatar or party movement brings them into and back out of the bubble repeatedly. For each member record last source position/coordinate resolution, admitted physical position or resumed OMT, persistent ID, goal and next eligible action, including the first actual movement or explained refusal. An abstract OMT can have several legal local placements. Check them against actual owner admission and terrain, preserving the precision the source supplies.
  - At every transfer exactly one owner has movement authority over each member. A successful native commit creates one event with previous/new owner and operation generation/epoch where supplied. A load, preflight, rejected attempt, copy or cached body cannot create that event. Multiple reads of a committed transfer produce the same result. Show local targets, pressure, casualties, changes of goal and members who remain under a different owner.
  - **Proof.** Run the offscreen leg and the repeated entry/exit case via the lab. Record authenticated IDs, useful route progress while remote, actual local NPC decisions in the city, both transfer directions more than once and the first moves after each. Compare the native per-member boundary facts with the spectator and replay. Include a map load with no transfer and an obstructed/refused or delayed first step. Flag disappearance, identity replacement, illegal jumps, doubled movement or travel that starts only after observation.

### Observation, replay and control

- [ ] 🔴 R-LAB-TRACE — Explain selected decisions and committed outcomes on their producing turn.
  - `src/raid_decision_trace.h` provides run-bound recording, actor/turn selection, bounded rows, repeat compaction and explicit truncation. `npc::trace_hostile_decision_for_harness` is selective. Extend or adapt this path at the native owners: NPC arbitration in `npc::move`, `npc::execute_action`, `npc::update_path`, `map::route`, monster decisions and abstract travel/commits. Join by run, game turn, actor, ordered decision site and action occurrence. Record the winning reason and relevant considered alternatives from operands the owner actually examined, plus target, intended step, route reuse/failure and actual result. Distinguish missing data, skipped evaluation, suppressed repeats and truncated windows. A failed route or a decision to move has a different observable result from a committed step.
  - NPC `character_id` and available persistent monster keys, e.g. `monster::handoff_actor_id()` for an identified predator, survive relevant boundaries. `Creature::get_identity()` originates in a process-local counter in `src/creature.cpp`. Bind other actors through an unambiguous reproducible encounter identity and lifecycle, including births/deaths if they occur. Never merge actors by screen label, position, species or pointer. Tracing reads values already decided and must preserve RNG, route/cache state, ordering and actor decisions.
  - **Proof.** Follow an NPC and a stalker/target through selected windows, match a repeated failed attempt and later success against positions or a real effect, and exercise a cap that reports incomplete detail. Re-run with diagnostics off and compare normalized behavior. Existing raid selections and output remain usable.

- [ ] 🔴 R-LAB-SPECTATE — Watch, pan, follow, pause and step in Tiles without changing who acts.
  - `game::draw_ter(const tripoint_bub_ms &, ...)` in `src/game.cpp` accepts a view center. Ordinary view paths also read avatar position, visibility and `view_offset`. The lab owns a display center, focus ID and playback cursor. Render loaded terrain and actors from the observed run, with an overmap view/position and last known local observation for remote actors. Never load a remote city to make a local scene for an abstract turn. Preserve independent faction attitudes and simulation knowledge even when the observer can see both sides.
  - Treat display input as read-only between completed turns. Repeated redraws, pause duration and follow changes must preserve the player, map origin, loaded set, RNG and next world outcome. `src/cata_tiles.cpp` uses `rng_bits()` in the weather tile branch. Account for presentation-only randomness and render side effects on the real route. Passive observation has no reason to invoke a debug character swap.
  - **Proof.** In a real Tiles session watch an independent party while the player stays elsewhere, pan and switch actors, pause for multiple redraws, single-step and resume. Verify the visible result and compare each turn with headless and a no-camera-input control, including a crossing, an offscreen turn and weather redraws. A missing/dead focus is reported without advancing or substituting a same-name actor.

- [ ] 🔴 R-LAB-REPLAY — Open a plotted hitch or wriggle in Tiles and localize the first divergence.
  - Each bookmark identifies immutable encounter revision, actual seed/start, builds/data/options, native game turn and ordered actors/events, plus a preceding turn window. Clicking it opens Tiles at the start of the available lead-in, allows follow/pause/step, and shows the original headless measurement and selected decisions through the marked turn. Recreate native gameplay from the original start, or a demonstrated equivalent checkpoint with RNG, clock, bodies, authority, queued inputs and required transient state restored. A save file alone does not establish that equivalence. State capture and replay cannot issue replacement AI commands.
  - Normalize native facts at every turn by stable actor identity, absolute coordinates/unit, live status, health/effects, applicable goal/target, custody, relevant action/outcome and world/route changes. Include meaningful action order while excluding timings, rendering and process identities. Compare semantic records as well as hashes. Show last common and first differing turn with actor, field/decision and expected/actual facts, including missing/new actors. A new seed or changed setup can diverge at initialization. Label an incomplete or incompatible reproduction with the first limit and last verified point.
  - **Proof.** Open both marker types from a real headless run, then visually follow the same actors through the marked turn. Reproduce unchanged semantic history even when a timing spike has a different duration. Compare a known single change and resolve its first unequal fact. A mismatched start or truncated record must never silently yield a matching replay. Keep the recipe and minimal reproducible data for a selected regression while cleaning disposable run output.

- [ ] 🔴 R-LAB-FOLLOWER — Support optional “Play as this follower” on an isolated playable branch.
  - `debug_menu::debug_menu_index::CONTROL_NPC` routes to `avatar::control_npc_menu(true)` and `avatar::control_npc(npc &, bool)` in `src/avatar.cpp`. The menu reads the follower list. The swap checks alliance, updates missions and follower membership, swaps character state, recenters the active map and emits the avatar change. Offer the action only for an eligible allied follower. Revalidate eligibility and identity at execution.
  - Create independent writable world state at the selected turn before invoking the normal debug swap. Bind subsequent player commands and saves to that branch. A canceled, stale or failed fork leaves the watched run at its original turn. An independent faction member or monster can be followed with the camera.
  - **Proof.** From a bookmarked run with an allied follower, fork, swap to its exact ID, act and save. Reopen the baseline and reproduce the original player, party, turn, measurements and later decisions. Exercise an unaffiliated actor, monster, and canceled fork; none obtains player control.

### Whole-turn timing and cost

- [ ] 🔴 R-LAB-PLOT — Plot every completed headless turn with adjustable budgets and nonblocking bookmarks.
  - `openclaw_harness_turn_trace` in `src/do_turn.cpp` currently starts after the clock transition. It subtracts intervals marked outside simulation, including the bracket around `handle_action()`. Real player actions can update the map there. Use a monotonic outer measurement around the complete advancing native turn in headless mode, including ordinary action execution, active overmap service, boundary loads and handoff work wherever it runs. Separate actual external input wait, UI/render, diagnostic report writing and startup from measured game work. Where an existing bracket includes both gameplay and excluded time, measure the inner boundary accurately or retain the inseparable game work and disclose it. Keep initial world construction/loading and non-advancing startup observations visible separately. An expensive first *advancing* turn remains on the main plot.
  - Show one ordered whole-turn series in milliseconds. Show the default **30 ms** warning line, effective thresholds, counts, median, p95, and worst value with its turn. Let the developer adjust whole-turn limits and apply tighter encounter or overmap-work limits; label the quantity each applies to. For a threshold expressed as a maximum, warn on values greater than the limit using unrounded samples. Evaluate all crossing turns. Show scenario/seed and matched before/after deltas for the same build conditions, actor workload and interval, with an explicit explanation for a mismatch. A deliberate code comparison records both revisions. Keep incomplete turns and missing samples visible. Avoid counting a turn twice when several budgets fire.
  - Overlay actual overmap update, dispatch, local NPC and monster work, map-load operations, and successful boundary transfers on the same timeline. A scheduler check with no work, a paid travel step and a new party dispatch have distinct meanings. Select any turn to inspect its owners, work and actors, and select a bookmark to replay. A warning records the turn and evaluation and lets the batch continue to its configured horizon or a genuine game/runner terminal condition.
  - **Proof.** Feed known durations and limits through the real report path to verify unit conversion, median/p95 convention, rounding, denominator, worst turn, totals, baseline delta and threshold equality. Exercise two native seeds, ordinary quiet turns, a due overmap tick, a turn with genuine dispatch, a crossing and an independently tighter budget. Lower a limit to produce a real bookmark, click it, and verify later turns/seeds complete. A headless timing point retains its original value after a slower Tiles replay or long spectator pause.

- [ ] 🔴 R-LAB-COST — Attribute whole-turn cost to executed work and quantify instrumentation overhead.
  - `raid_perf_scope`, `raid_perf_row`, `write_raid_perf_row` and the `raid_perf_stage` sections in `src/do_turn.cpp` already measure nested stages, counters and calls. For example `creatures` contains monster work, NPC preparation and NPC actions; route and item work can nest deeper. `map::load` in `src/map.cpp` is an operation on map data. Its call count is independent of a committed change of movement authority. Preserve existing run bindings and diagnostic modes.
  - Bind per-turn timings to the actual call/commit, clock kind, owner and parent interval. Display inclusive elapsed time for a scope and exclusive elapsed time after subtracting the union of measured child intervals on that timeline. Reconcile disjoint accounted wall intervals plus explicit unattributed time to the measured whole within timer resolution. Keep overlapping views, CPU time and work on other threads separate. Show unknown coverage or inconsistent boundaries directly. A graph reader must be able to inspect an unexplained remainder.
  - Count executed work where the owner performs it: e.g. active actors/actions, route requests/reuse/results, candidate scans, map locations/loads, abstract travel or dispatch work, and handoffs by direction. Show the relevant units, including repeated calls to one location. Distinguish a lazy load, generation, save, actor preparation and materialization when evidence supports each. Never infer disk time, map generation or successful actor transfer from a parent scope alone. Record real overmap work and dispatch outcomes with site/party and members, and the local NPC/monster stages on that same turn.
  - Match diagnostic-on and diagnostic-off headless runs on input, build configuration, machine, seed/start, game turns and work achieved. Keep the cheap whole-turn measurement or an equivalent external boundary in the off run. Attribute instrumentation's change in median/p95/worst and warn about run variation. Use deterministic work counts to compare workloads where available; disabled detailed counters are unavailable, not zero. Do not make noisy timing alone a behavioral failure.
  - **World 2 reference.** The 2026-11-23 dispatch map-load replay in [debug findings](../debug-findings.md) reports minute 7800, game turn 5220000 at 1080.166 ms wall, 157.456 ms CPU and 1039.621 ms inclusive overmap NPC time. Four OMT loads across two site preparations had zero materialization events. The two preparation intervals leave about 196 ms inside the overmap stage. Bandit members 10/12 and cannibal members 11/14 subsequently moved. The historical >100 ms filter and timing values belong to that build/run. Other recorded overmap hitches had zero observed map loads.
  - **Proof.** Use the World 2 reference to validate the distinctions and a source-bound headless replay or comparable native dispatch workload to demonstrate the live report. Bind the actual workload and any difference from the reference. Drill from the plot into dispatch, members, map work and residual, then check later same-member movement. Show a genuine handoff elsewhere, a quiet tick and a costly local actor turn. Reconcile totals and compare detailed/off runs on matched work. A particularly fast run can pass if the report still attributes the real work; recreating a historical millisecond value is unnecessary.

## Acceptance through normal use

1. **Build a case and run a matrix.** From the lab entry, save a seeded stalker case, add pressure or an obstacle, rerun both configurations and compare state and cost. Repeat the unmodified case from a fresh process. This covers R-LAB-ENCOUNTER, R-LAB-TURN and R-LAB-STALKER. The existing stalker suite in `tests/writhing_stalker_test.cpp` and selected fixtures provide focused lower-level controls.
2. **Follow a city caravan.** Keep the party offscreen throughout one leg, then exercise ordinary entry, exit and reentry on a separate case. Inspect individual IDs, actual positions, goals and first actions. Compare against the native game owner with the camera moved, paused and stepped. This covers R-LAB-CARAVAN and R-LAB-SPECTATE. `tests/bandit_live_world_test.cpp` includes mixed-ownership, refused admission, repeated crossings and unsaved continuity examples for specialized parties.
3. **Investigate from the plot.** Select one genuine performance warning and one movement warning, play both preceding windows in Tiles, then compare a changed variant. Check actor decisions and first semantic divergence as well as diagnostic-off timing and workload. This covers R-LAB-PLOT, R-LAB-COST, R-LAB-TRACE and R-LAB-REPLAY. Reuse `tests/native_performance_extrema_test.cpp`, `tests/native_slow_turn_receipts_test.cpp` and `tools/openclaw_harness/decision_trace_view_test.py` for narrower failure cases.
4. **Intervene and return.** Branch at a known turn, play an eligible follower and revisit the intact baseline. Verify rejected selections and subsequent baseline replay for R-LAB-FOLLOWER.

Record source/build, effective recipe, seed, run and turn identities with the result. Exercise supported headless and graphical builds on the repo's supported platforms during delivery. A test-only turn, forced actor motion, a screenshot without the corresponding advancing gameplay, or a detached chart cannot settle the connected workflow.
