# Three calls on channel 27 — round three

Luna heard the same five player transmissions in all three calls, with the same character, inventory and overmap. The food offer and player dialogue are new for this round. Each voice received its own full prior exchange and one of the three revised private role briefs. The letters were shuffled independently for this round.

## Conversation A

**You** — Hello? I've got a tin of peaches to spare. Been a while since I heard another voice on here. Anybody listening?

**A** — Hello. I’m listening—thanks, a tin of peaches would be a welcome change. Would you meet me at the campground east of Ansonia and bring that tin?

**You** — Just the one tin. Call me Finch. Nothing in return. Yesterday was my brother's birthday, and I didn't much feel like eating his favorite peaches by myself. You ever have a day like that?

**A** — Finch, I’m sorry—birthdays can make an empty chair feel louder than usual; yes, I’ve had days like that. Nothing in return, then—would you meet me at the campground east of Ansonia with that one tin?

**You** — Still sealed. I said I wanted nothing, but that's not quite true. I'd like ten minutes of company. Does that change things for you?

**A** — It changes things in the best way; ten minutes of company sounds good, and you don’t have to open the tin. Let’s meet at the campground east of Ansonia.

**You** — He could whistle through a blade of grass. Drove me nuts. I'd give anything to hear it now. Your turn. Tell me one thing about yourself I couldn't guess from your voice.

**A** — Before all this, I was a hardware-store clerk. I used to sort loose screws by touch without looking—my hands remember better than I do.

**You** — He'd sing the price tags out loud at the checkout. Terrible voice. Listen, I'd be glad to give you the tin. Are we meeting somewhere, or would you rather keep this on the radio?

**A** — The campground east of Ansonia still sounds good; we can share those ten minutes there, and I’d be grateful for the peaches.

## Conversation B

**You** — Hello? I've got a tin of peaches to spare. Been a while since I heard another voice on here. Anybody listening?

**B** — Hello. I’m listening, but I don’t trade a meeting for a promise over the radio. What are you hoping to get in return for the peaches?

**You** — Just the one tin. Call me Finch. Nothing in return. Yesterday was my brother's birthday, and I didn't much feel like eating his favorite peaches by myself. You ever have a day like that?

**B** — I’m sorry, Finch. Yeah—some days a familiar thing feels more like a reminder than a meal; is the tin still sealed?

**You** — Still sealed. I said I wanted nothing, but that's not quite true. I'd like ten minutes of company. Does that change things for you?

**B** — It changes what I’m willing to offer, not my caution about meeting; we can keep each other company over the radio for ten minutes. Tell me something about your brother that has nothing to do with the peaches.

**You** — He could whistle through a blade of grass. Drove me nuts. I'd give anything to hear it now. Your turn. Tell me one thing about yourself I couldn't guess from your voice.

**B** — I didn't work a very interesting job—hardware store clerk—but I could tell you which odd screw or hinge someone needed just by looking at it; never did learn to whistle through grass, though. What’s one small thing your brother used to do that still catches you off guard?

**You** — He'd sing the price tags out loud at the checkout. Terrible voice. Listen, I'd be glad to give you the tin. Are we meeting somewhere, or would you rather keep this on the radio?

**B** — That sounds like the kind of ridiculous thing you miss more than you expect; I’d rather keep this on the radio for now. What tune did he use for the price tags—and what did he sing when the line got long?

## Conversation C

**You** — Hello? I've got a tin of peaches to spare. Been a while since I heard another voice on here. Anybody listening?

**C** — Hello. I could use those peaches—let’s talk at the campground east of Ansonia, in daylight; you bringing just the one tin?

**You** — Just the one tin. Call me Finch. Nothing in return. Yesterday was my brother's birthday, and I didn't much feel like eating his favorite peaches by myself. You ever have a day like that?

**C** — I have, Finch; some days the quiet makes a small kindness feel heavier than it ought to. The campground east of Ansonia, in daylight, works for me—just the one tin is fine.

**You** — Still sealed. I said I wanted nothing, but that's not quite true. I'd like ten minutes of company. Does that change things for you?

**C** — No, it doesn’t change things. Ten minutes of company sounds all right; we’ll meet at the campground east of Ansonia in daylight, and the sealed tin is plenty.

**You** — He could whistle through a blade of grass. Drove me nuts. I'd give anything to hear it now. Your turn. Tell me one thing about yourself I couldn't guess from your voice.

**C** — I used to sort every loose screw that came across the hardware counter by size, even when nobody asked me to. Guess I liked making one small thing orderly.

**You** — He'd sing the price tags out loud at the checkout. Terrible voice. Listen, I'd be glad to give you the tin. Are we meeting somewhere, or would you rather keep this on the radio?

**C** — Let’s meet at the campground east of Ansonia in daylight, then. I’d rather share those ten minutes of company in person—just bring the one tin.
