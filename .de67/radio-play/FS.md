## The player’s journey

Use a walkie-talkie, hear **Voice** on channel 27, and ask what happened. A traveler says they need antibiotics and suggests a campground east of a town. Check the known overmap, return to a half-written reply and agree to the offer. The journal records what to bring and lets the player reread the directions. Bring the goods to the same person, on the road or at the campground. They can accept what actually changed hands and decide in person whether to join the player.

The game also lets a bandit, a cannibal, a Mi-go or a portal storm sound like an unknown voice. Their in-world identities determine their behavior. The player judges them by what is audible and what happens in the world. All contact, travel and settlement claims below are open. A dialogue experiment or working component alone earns no radio gameplay credit.

## Sources of truth

- **Frequency and audibility.** Give each eligible human endpoint a persistent NPC ID, camp affiliation when applicable, channel and switch schedule. A Mi-go receiver binds to its real site and population identity. A storm binds to its live event instance. The simulation produces availability. Dial, chat, star, map and callbacks are readers. Keep the player's channel history keyed to the avatar and frequency. Keep calls and heard turns keyed to participant identity. Two users of channel 27 can have entirely different histories.
- **World and player saves.** A suitable new durable home for contact schedules and call records is `overmap_global_state` in `src/overmapbuffer.h`. Its serialization in `src/savegame.cpp` runs through `game::serialize_master` and `game::unserialize_master`, alongside world missions. Namespace player-owned records by character ID. Store physical NPC, party and mission truth in their native owners and reference them by stable IDs. Rebuild transient indexes from stored records. Missing fields in older saves begin empty, existing people receive frequencies before discovery, and invalid references cannot select a replacement speaker.
- **What an utterance can do.** The game thread commits a send with call, speaker, exchange ID, tuned frequency, game turn, actual sender tile and receivers at send time. Append an audible line once, set the player's star on their send, and notify every eligible listener once. Record actual receivers even when a model call or reply fails. A private suffix may publish a pending proposal. Only the player's explicit acceptance can authorize a mission or party. Only a physical transaction settles goods. A late backend result carries its originating call, speaker, turn and offer revision. Revalidate those before publishing.
- **Who moves.** The existing lone-NPC scheduler, camp outing or Mi-go operation owns the relevant actor, route and reservations. Radio supplies a validated destination or observation. Local perception and combat take precedence on loaded tiles. Transfer authority across the boundary using the same actor ID, physical position and outing generation. An unavailable actor, superseded order or already-resolved outcome cannot be revived by a callback.
- **What the player knows.** Persist private identity, coordinates, goals and outcomes independently of public aliases, conversation turns and disclosed mission status. Every UI reader renders only the latter. Ordinary debug messages follow the same rule. Deliberately opened diagnostics can expose game provenance and may never print credential values.
