# Three calls on channel 27 — round two

Luna received each voice’s complete spoken history on every turn. After each set of three replies, the player sent the same response to all three. The private roles were shuffled anew for this round.

## Conversation A

**You** — I've got two clean bandages I could spare. Could you use them?

**Voice A** — I could use those—are they still individually wrapped? Could you bring the two to the campground east of Ansonia?

**You** — Real first-aid bandages, both still sealed. I keep them in a blue tea tin. I can bring a liter of clean water too. What would you want me to set aside?

**Voice A** — Set aside both sealed bandages and the liter of water—what’s it stored in, and is the container sealed? Bring them to the campground east of Ansonia.

**You** — An old soda bottle. I washed it, boiled the water this morning, and screwed the cap back on. That's as good as I've got. Anything you want to know about the person offering it?

**Voice A** — An old bottle with a screw cap isn’t sealed—how much water does it hold, and are you coming alone? I still need the two wrapped bandages, too.

**You** — Call me Finch. It's a full liter. Soap and hot water for the cap, but I didn't boil the cap itself. I keep my travel plans off the air. As for why I'm offering, somebody did the same for me last winter. Fair enough?

**Voice A** — Fair enough, Finch. I’ll take the liter and both sealed bandages, though I won’t count the bottle as sterile; meet me at the campground east of Ansonia.

**You** — Scrubbed and rinsed the inside too, yes. I'm keeping the tin I mentioned, though. Pencil's out now. What am I putting in my pack for you, and what place am I writing down?

**Voice A** — Pack the 2 sealed bandages and 1 liter of water; I’m not counting the bottle as sterile. Write down the campground east of Ansonia.

## Conversation B

**You** — I've got two clean bandages I could spare. Could you use them?

**Voice B** — Thanks, but I’m not hurt right now. Are those individually wrapped and sterile, or just clean cloth? I could use drinking water more than bandages.

**You** — Real first-aid bandages, both still sealed. I keep them in a blue tea tin. I can bring a liter of clean water too. What would you want me to set aside?

**Voice B** — The liter of water would help most; thanks for clarifying about the bandages. Is the water in a clean, sealed container? I’d want to know how it’s been kept before arranging anything.

**You** — An old soda bottle. I washed it, boiled the water this morning, and screwed the cap back on. That's as good as I've got. Anything you want to know about the person offering it?

**Voice B** — Boiling helps, but I’m not sure about the bottle or the cap—how did you clean them, and did you boil the cap too? I don’t know you yet; what should I call you?

**You** — Call me Finch. It's a full liter. Soap and hot water for the cap, but I didn't boil the cap itself. I keep my travel plans off the air. As for why I'm offering, somebody did the same for me last winter. Fair enough?

**Voice B** — Fair enough, Finch—thanks for paying it forward. I’m still wary of the old bottle; did you scrub it with soap and rinse it well too, or just wash it out?

**You** — Scrubbed and rinsed the inside too, yes. I'm keeping the tin I mentioned, though. Pencil's out now. What am I putting in my pack for you, and what place am I writing down?

**Voice B** — That helps; keep the tin, then—please pack the liter of water and both sealed bandages. We can meet at the campground east of Ansonia; I’m still a little wary of that bottle, so keep it capped.

## Conversation C

**You** — I've got two clean bandages I could spare. Could you use them?

**Voice C** — I’m not hurt, so I don’t have much use for bandages right now. Are they individually wrapped? A liter of drinking water would help me more.

**You** — Real first-aid bandages, both still sealed. I keep them in a blue tea tin. I can bring a liter of clean water too. What would you want me to set aside?

**Voice C** — A liter of clean water would help; please set that aside, and keep the sealed bandages too. What’s the water in—has it been kept in a clean, covered container?

**You** — An old soda bottle. I washed it, boiled the water this morning, and screwed the cap back on. That's as good as I've got. Anything you want to know about the person offering it?

**Voice C** — That’s better than an open container. How much water is in the bottle, and did you keep the inside and cap clean while filling it?

**You** — Call me Finch. It's a full liter. Soap and hot water for the cap, but I didn't boil the cap itself. I keep my travel plans off the air. As for why I'm offering, somebody did the same for me last winter. Fair enough?

**Voice C** — A full liter helps, and washing the cap with soap and hot water sounds reasonable; I’d still rather not drink from a bottle if the inside wasn’t clean too. Fair enough—let’s meet at the motel northwest of Piermont, and bring the liter of water.

**You** — Scrubbed and rinsed the inside too, yes. I'm keeping the tin I mentioned, though. Pencil's out now. What am I putting in my pack for you, and what place am I writing down?

**Voice C** — Good, that eases my concern about the bottle. Bring the liter of water and both sealed bandages, without the tin; write down the motel northwest of Piermont.
