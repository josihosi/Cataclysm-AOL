# Three calls on channel 27 — round four

A cautious player opens with a greeting and identifies no goods. All three Luna calls used the same five player transmissions, character, inventory and map. Each voice saw only its own prior exchange. The letters were freshly shuffled.

## Conversation A

**You** — Hello? Who is this? Who am I talking to?

**A** — Robin here. Just a traveler trying to get by—who am I speaking with?

**You** — I'd rather keep my name to myself for now. I'll listen, Robin. What made you get on the radio today?

**A** — I’m checking whether anyone out there is still willing to trade—clean water’s getting harder to count on, especially in this rain. What are you hoping to find on the radio?

**You** — I wanted to know who else was out there. Say I haven't got anything to spare. Would you still want to talk?

**A** — Yeah, I’d still talk—doesn’t take anything to listen. What were you hoping to find out there, besides whether someone had supplies to spare?

**You** — I'm staying on the radio. I've heard things out there that sound like people. How do I know you're not one of them?

**A** — You can’t know for sure from a voice alone, and I won’t ask you to take my word for it. What did you hear that sounded like people—and what made you decide to answer me anyway?

**You** — It kept saying “hold the line” exactly the same way. You actually answered me. Maybe I'll call again tomorrow. If I do, what's the one thing you need most?

**A** — If you call again, clean drinking water would help most—the rain isn’t much good for that. And I’m glad you answered; what made you trust that it was really me on the other end?

## Conversation B

**You** — Hello? Who is this? Who am I talking to?

**B** — This is Robin. Just a survivor trying to get by—who am I speaking with?

**You** — I'd rather keep my name to myself for now. I'll listen, Robin. What made you get on the radio today?

**B** — I was hoping to find someone willing to trade for food or drinking water; the rain hasn’t made either any easier to come by. I’m not asking you to trust me—just tell me what you might spare.

**You** — I wanted to know who else was out there. Say I haven't got anything to spare. Would you still want to talk?

**B** — Yeah, I’d still talk. I’m not great at trusting strangers, but it’d be good to know there’s someone else out there—what are you hoping to find?

**You** — I'm staying on the radio. I've heard things out there that sound like people. How do I know you're not one of them?

**B** — That’s fair—I can’t prove what I am through a speaker, and I won’t ask you to take my word for it. We can keep talking from a distance, and you can decide for yourself whether I sound like a person.

**You** — It kept saying “hold the line” exactly the same way. You actually answered me. Maybe I'll call again tomorrow. If I do, what's the one thing you need most?

**B** — That “hold the line” business sounds wrong; I’m glad you kept your distance from it. If you call tomorrow, drinking water’s what I need most—the rain’s everywhere, but none of it’s safe to drink.

## Conversation C

**You** — Hello? Who is this? Who am I talking to?

**C** — This is Robin. I’m just trying to get by out here—are you friendly?

**You** — I'd rather keep my name to myself for now. I'll listen, Robin. What made you get on the radio today?

**C** — The rain’s been coming down, and I couldn’t stand sitting alone with the radio silence anymore. I’m not hurt, but I’m running low on food and water—could you spare a little if we find a safe way to meet?

**You** — I wanted to know who else was out there. Say I haven't got anything to spare. Would you still want to talk?

**C** — Yes, I’d still want to talk; I’m sorry, I didn’t mean to make you feel you had to bring anything. If you’re comfortable, maybe we could meet at the campground east of Ansonia—somewhere public, where we can talk without being alone in the rain.

**You** — I'm staying on the radio. I've heard things out there that sound like people. How do I know you're not one of them?

**C** — I don’t know how to prove it over the radio, but I’m scared too; I’m just a person trying to make it through the rain. You don’t owe me a meeting or anything—what did you hear out there?

**You** — It kept saying “hold the line” exactly the same way. You actually answered me. Maybe I'll call again tomorrow. If I do, what's the one thing you need most?

**C** — I’m glad you called back; hearing that same “hold the line” message over and over would’ve scared me too. If you call tomorrow, clean drinking water would help me most—but I’d still be glad just to hear a real voice.
